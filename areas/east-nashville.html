Text Maya about East Nashville

East Nashville sits across the Cumberland River from downtown, bounded roughly by the river to the south and west, Ellington Parkway on the west side, and Gallatin Pike running up the middle. It is the largest of the neighborhoods I work and the one I know best, because I live in it. I bought on Holly Street in 2019, and most of what I know about the east side I learned by walking to Five Points and back.

What the housing looks like

The core of 37206 was built between about 1900 and 1940: craftsman bungalows, American four-squares, shotgun cottages, and a few Victorians closest to the river in Edgefield. Lots are typically 50 feet wide. Many houses have been renovated once already, some well and some badly, which is why I spend more time in attics and crawlspaces here than anywhere else. North of Eastland Avenue and into 37216 the housing shifts to 1940s and 1950s brick ranches and cottages on bigger lots, and that's where you still find room under the median.

The other thing you'll see everywhere is infill. Since about 2012, builders have bought single lots and put two tall, narrow houses on them. They range from very good to very thin, and the price per square foot is usually the lowest in the neighborhood. If you're weighing one, I'll walk you through the shared-driveway agreement and the HOA, if there is one, before you write an offer.

What it costs

The East Nashville market report from RealTracs puts the median sold price at about $556,600 and the median list price near $585,900 as of September 2026. On the day I wrote this, the active listings on this page ran from $293,900 for a two-bedroom condo at Solo East on Litton Avenue to $1,040,000 for a five-bedroom on Riverwood Drive in Eastdale Place. A renovated three-bedroom bungalow in Lockeland Springs typically lands between $700,000 and $950,000. The same house in Inglewood, ten minutes north, is often $150,000 less.

Getting around

Downtown is about ten minutes by car over the Woodland Street bridge. The airport is about eighteen minutes on I-40 East. If you'd rather not drive, the Shelby Bottoms greenway connects to the Cumberland River pedestrian bridge and drops you at Nissan Stadium in about twenty minutes of easy riding. WeGo bus routes run up Gallatin Pike and Main Street.

Parks

Shelby Park is the anchor: about 300 acres along the river with a public golf course, the dog park, ballfields, and the Shelby Bottoms Nature Center. Shelby Bottoms itself is roughly 950 acres of floodplain and paved trail. East Park on Woodland has the community center and pool. Smaller pocket parks show up all over Lockeland Springs and Eastwood.

Food and the rest

Five Points, at Woodland Street and 11th Street, is the commercial heart: Five Points Pizza, Mas Tacos Por Favor, Rosepepper Cantina, Lockeland Table a few blocks up. Riverside Village, on McGavock Pike in Inglewood, is the quieter second center with Mitchell Delicatessen. Tomato Art Fest takes over Five Points the second Saturday of every August; if you're touring houses that weekend, plan around it.

What to watch for

Three things I check on every east-side house. First, the flood map: the 2010 flood reached parts of the neighborhood near the river and Shelby Bottoms, and I want you to know a house's zone before you make an offer. Second, wiring: some "fully renovated" bungalows still have original knob-and-tube in the attic. Third, the rail line: the CSX tracks run through the east side and a few otherwise perfect streets back up to them. None of these are dealbreakers. All of them belong in the price.

Neighborhoods and pockets

  • Lockeland Springs Where I live. Craftsman bungalows and four-squares on tree streets between Eastland and Woodland; the most-asked-for pocket and priced like it.
  • Five Points The commercial heart at Woodland and 11th. Walk to everything; expect smaller lots and more traffic on weekend nights.
  • Historic Edgefield Closest to the river and the bridge. Victorians and cottages, the oldest housing stock on the east side.
  • Inglewood North of Eastland toward Gallatin Pike. Bigger yards, 1940s and 50s brick, and the most room left in the price band.
  • Eastwood Neighbors Quiet grid between Porter Road and Gallatin. Mid-century ranches and a lot of new infill.
  • Cleveland Park and McFerrin Park West of Ellington Parkway. Shotgun houses, new builds, and prices still under the east-side average.
  • Joywood Heights and Shadow Lawn Up by Riverside Village in 37216. Two of the listings on this page are here.

Around East Nashville

  • Shelby Park About 300 acres on the river with the golf course, ballfields, and the dog park.
  • Shelby Bottoms Greenway Roughly 950 acres of floodplain trail; you can bike to downtown on it.
  • Five Points Five Points Pizza, Mas Tacos, Rosepepper, and the bars everybody ends up at.
  • Riverside Village The other commercial pocket, up on McGavock Pike, with Mitchell Deli and a proper hardware store.
  • Tomato Art Fest Second Saturday of August in Five Points. The whole neighborhood turns out.
  • East Park and the East Branch Library The community center on Woodland, plus a pool.
  • Lockeland Table and Margot The two dinners I send visiting relatives to.

Questions about East Nashville

What does a house in East Nashville cost right now?

The median sold price in the East Nashville market report is about $556,600 as of September 2026, with listings on the market from the low $300,000s for a two-bedroom to just over $1,000,000 for a renovated five-bedroom. Lockeland Springs and Edgefield run above the median; Inglewood, Cleveland Park, and 37207 run below it.

How long is the commute to downtown?

About ten minutes by car over the Woodland Street or Korean Veterans bridge, and 20 to 25 minutes by bike on the Shelby Bottoms greenway to the pedestrian bridge.

Which schools serve East Nashville?

Metro Nashville Public Schools. Zoning depends on the exact address; the RealTracs market page lists Tom Joy Elementary, Jere Baxter Middle, Maplewood High, and Stratford STEM Magnet among the schools serving the area. Use the MNPS zone finder for any specific house.

Does East Nashville flood?

Parts of it did in May 2010, mostly near the river and along Shelby Bottoms. Ask me about any specific street; I keep the FEMA map open during showings and I'll tell you if a house sits in a flood zone before you fall in love with it.

Is there new construction?

Yes, mostly tall-and-skinny two-unit builds on subdivided lots, especially in Cleveland Park, McFerrin Park, and Inglewood. They're often the cheapest way into the area per square foot. The trade-off is a shared driveway and a small yard.

What's the difference between 37206 and 37216?

37206 is the historic core: Lockeland Springs, Five Points, Edgefield, closer to downtown and pricier. 37216 is Inglewood and Riverside Village, a bit farther out with larger lots and more mid-century houses.
